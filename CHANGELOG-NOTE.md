- **v0.10.0-draft (2026-05-24):** Heaviest restructure since v0.6.0. Adds Section 4D (temporal evolution and substrate currency) as a third architectural axis paralleling 4A (patient substrate), 4B (institutional substrate), and 4C (interaction semantics). The motivation, in the user's framing during the design discussion: "life happens, things changes (more often is so rapidly, silent ignorant is what usually kills patients unintentionally)." Clinical evidence, guidelines, formularies, and institutional policies evolve continuously, and an architecture that ignores this is architecturally complicit in patient harm because silent staleness is invisible to clinicians who trust the system. Section 4D introduces five principles (versioned substrate components, evidence currency as first-class signal, sound evolution under operator-set change, clinician-mediated propagation of change, evolution-aware provenance) — paralleling the five-principle structure of 4A and 4B. The load-bearing safety claim of 4D is 4D.4: silent updates to active care are architecturally forbidden; guideline transitions propagate only through explicit clinician re-review events. The principles integrate with four verified standards and regulatory frameworks (HL7 FHIR Clinical Reasoning Module and CQL, Cochrane Living Systematic Reviews and MAGICapp, FDA Predetermined Change Control Plans, EU AI Act Article 72 post-market monitoring), each documented in a new Section 6 sub-section with [verified] tags. Section 7 gains 7E.6, a worked example using the live SSC 2021 → SSC 2026 transition that occurred in March 2026 (Prescott et al., Crit Care Med, doi:10.1097/CCM.0000000000007075, 129 statements with 46 new); the example walks through each 4D principle in action as the substrate handles 40 active sepsis patients during the transition window. The TL;DR is extended to acknowledge the third temporal axis. Section 3's architectural commitment is extended to three coupled elements. Section 4's intro is rewritten to mention four sub-sections; a leftover duplicate Section 4 header from an earlier version is cleaned up in passing. Section 5 retains its structure (the system-level identity does not change shape with the addition of 4D, but Section 5 now implicitly addresses the temporal dimension through 4D). The synthesis claim in Section 6 is extended to mention 4D and the four integrated standards; the residual originality claim does not change shape but the four-contribution framing now sits alongside the temporal-evolution standards integration as a fifth area where the architecture engages with the broader landscape. Section 8 gains a new limitation: the four temporal-evolution standards are mature individually but their integration into a deployed clinical substrate is engineering work not yet done at scale, part of the 2–4 year deployment horizon and possibly larger than the Section 7 timeline narrative previously implied. References section gains 9 new entries (Prescott 2026 SSC, KDIGO 2024 CKD, KDIGO AKI scope-of-work, HL7 FHIR Clinical Reasoning Module, CQL 1.5.3, Cochrane LSR Guidance combined with MAGICapp and Australian Living Evidence Collaboration, FDA PCCP final guidance, FDA/Health Canada/MHRA joint principles, EU AI Act Article 72). The anti-example's purpose is to make the architecture's scope-claim visible and falsifiable: substrate-first is a strong fit for clinical domains with crisp lattices, direct individual-level evidence, informative abstention, and signal-carrying institutional layer; it is not a fit for clinical domains without these. The depression anti-example is representative, not exhaustive — other limitations of scope likely exist and may surface in future versions. Chronic depression was chosen over oncology staging as the anti-example because oncology staging fits the architecture too well (TNM is lattice-shaped, NCCN guidelines are deduction-shaped); a poor-fit example carries more scope-clarifying value than an unexpectedly good-fit example.
